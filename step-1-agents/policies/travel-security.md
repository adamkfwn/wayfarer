# Aid Nordic travel security policy

Document TS-2026-02. Owner: Global Security Adviser. Applies to all staff and consultants travelling on Aid Nordic business.

## 1. Purpose

Aid Nordic staff travel frequently between headquarters in Copenhagen and Stockholm and country programmes in Kenya, Jordan, Bangladesh and Colombia, and onward to field locations. This policy sets out what is required before, during and after travel so that staff are safe and the organisation's systems stay protected.

## 2. Travel approval

### 2.1 Approval before departure

All international travel and all domestic travel to field locations must be approved before departure. Approval is given by the country director of the traveller's home programme, or by the Copenhagen country director for headquarters staff and for country directors themselves.

### 2.2 Travel register

Approved trips are recorded in the travel register with destination, start date, end date and approver. The register is the source of truth used by IT Security to explain sign-ins from unusual locations. A trip that is not in the register is treated as if it did not happen.

### 2.3 Changes to a trip

If a trip is extended, shortened or rerouted, the traveller or their line manager updates the register within one working day. Sign-ins outside the registered dates or destination will trigger identity alerts and may require the traveller to re-authenticate.

## 3. Devices

### 3.1 Registered devices only

Work systems may be accessed only from a device registered in Aid Nordic's device management and reported compliant. Personal phones and laptops must not be used for work data, including email, even while travelling.

### 3.2 Compliance before travel

Travellers check that their device shows as compliant before departure. A non-compliant device may be blocked from signing in at any time. IT can issue a loan device for the trip if the traveller's device cannot be brought into compliance.

### 3.3 Lost or seized devices

A lost, stolen or seized device is reported to IT Security immediately. IT Security will revoke the device's sessions and may reset the traveller's password. The traveller should expect to re-authenticate on their remaining devices.

## 4. Authentication while travelling

### 4.1 MFA

All staff use multi-factor authentication. The authenticator app or a hardware key is required; SMS codes are being phased out and are not accepted in high-risk duty stations. Travellers should register a second method before departure in case their phone is unavailable.

### 4.2 Unexpected prompts

Staff must not approve an MFA prompt they did not initiate. Repeated unexpected prompts are reported to IT Security the same day, as they indicate that the password is known to someone else.

### 4.3 Re-authentication requests

Staff should expect to be asked to re-authenticate when arriving in a new country or when signing in from a new device. This is normal and is not a sign that something is wrong.

## 5. Networks and VPN

### 5.1 Aid Nordic VPN

Travellers connect through the Aid Nordic VPN on untrusted networks such as hotel and airport wifi. The Aid Nordic VPN egress is recognised by IT Security and does not trigger location alerts.

### 5.2 Commercial VPNs

Commercial VPN services are discouraged. Where a duty station requires one for access to work systems, the traveller informs IT Security in advance so that the alert can be explained. Unannounced VPN use will be queried by an analyst.

## 6. After travel

The traveller confirms their return in the travel register. Sign-ins from the destination within 48 hours of the registered end date are treated as delayed departure and will require re-authentication only. Beyond 48 hours the trip is considered closed and the register should be corrected.

## 7. Contact

IT Security is reachable around the clock through the security duty phone. Country directors have the number. Travellers in doubt should call rather than wait.
